**openDuT Committer Candidate Guidelines**

To support a transparent, merit-based, and vendor-neutral growth of the openDuT community, contributors may be considered as candidates for a Committer Election after they have demonstrated sustained and valuable contributions to the project.

Typical indicators include one or more of the following:

- Contributing code, bug fixes, or new features
- Reviewing pull requests and providing constructive technical feedback
- Improving documentation, examples, or project infrastructure
- Participating regularly in community meetings and technical discussions
- Contributing to release preparation and testing activities
- Helping other community members by answering questions and supporting onboarding
- Participating in architecture discussions, roadmap planning, or project governance topics
- Demonstrating a good understanding of Eclipse Foundation processes and open source collaboration principles

The project values both technical and non-technical contributions. The overall impact on the project and the trust gained within the community are considered more important than any specific number of commits, pull requests, or issues.

Meeting these guidelines does not automatically result in Committer status. Committers are elected through the Eclipse Foundation election process based on demonstrated merit, community trust, and the judgement of the existing committers.

The purpose of these guidelines is to provide transparency regarding the types of contributions that are typically considered when evaluating potential Committer candidates.
